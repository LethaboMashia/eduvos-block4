# Interaction Design Analysis — Spotify

## Conceptual Model
Spotify gives you the mental picture of a huge personal music shelf that you can reach from anywhere. Songs live in a library, and you group them into playlists, which is borrowed straight from the physical mixtape and record collection. You think in terms of "my music" and "my playlists", not files, folders or servers, so you never need to know how streaming actually works behind the scenes.

## Principle 1 Demonstrated Well: Feedback
When I tap the heart icon on a song, it instantly turns green and a small message pops up saying the song was added to Liked Songs. I don't have to wonder whether my tap registered or go check my library to confirm. The response is immediate and clear, which makes the action feel safe and satisfying.

## Principle 2 Demonstrated Well: Visibility
The mini player at the bottom of the screen always shows the current song, the artist and a play/pause button, no matter which page I'm on. The most important controls stay in view the whole time, so I can pause or check what's playing without hunting for it. I always know the state of the app at a glance.

## Principle Violated: Affordance
On mobile, you can swipe a song to the right to add it to your queue, but nothing on the screen hints that this gesture exists. The row looks like a plain, static list item, so most people never discover it unless someone tells them. A feature that powerful has no visual clue that it can be used.

## What I'd Change
I would add a small visible queue icon next to each song, or show a one-time hint animation the first time a user opens a playlist, so the swipe gesture becomes discoverable.
